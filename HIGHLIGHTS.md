# Highlights

- **Overcoming single-model structural bias (Gap G1):** Prior TWS attribution studies rely on a single physics-based hydrological model (e.g., VIC, PCR-GLOBWB) whose structural assumptions propagate unconstrained biases. We introduce a novel **Twin Random Forest Attribution Framework** — a purely data-driven approach that disentangles natural hydroclimate variability from anthropogenic water abstraction without dependence on any single model's structural assumptions, applied across the world's 103 largest river basins.

- **First systematic global-scale driver separation (Gap G2):** Existing attribution studies are confined to individual basins or single regions (e.g., Indus, California, Middle East). Our framework provides the first **systematic, basin-by-basin driver attribution at unprecedented global scale** (103 basins, 0.5° × 0.5°, 213-month record), producing a comprehensive global map of dominant TWS-decline drivers — enabling cross-regime comparison of climate versus human forcing for the first time.

- **Quantitative anthropogenic fingerprinting (Gaps G1 + G2):** Where prior studies provide qualitative assessments of human impact, our **Variance Explained Gain (ΔR²)** metric — derived from contrasting a Natural Baseline Model (P, ET, Q) against a Full Anthropogenic Model (P, ET, Q, GW_abs, SW_abs) — provides a basin-specific, quantitative fingerprint of human-induced water depletion intensity that is independent of any hydrological model's structure.

- **Amplifying weak anthropogenic signals (Gap G5):** Standard ML implementations split on high-variance climate variables ($P$, $ET$), systematically burying subtle groundwater abstraction signals that vary by fractions of a cm/month. Our **forced feature-sampling strategy** (`NumPredictorsToSample = 1`) deliberately compels the Random Forest ensemble to build decision trees using each predictor — including $GW_{abs}$ and $SW_{abs}$ — overcoming the variance-magnitude imbalance that otherwise masks critical anthropogenic contributions.

- **Leakage-proof validation (Gap G3):** Many ML-based hydrological studies use random K-fold CV, which leaks temporally correlated information and inflates skill metrics. Our **leakage-aware validation protocol** — combining 3-year contiguous block cross-validation with a fixed-baseline (2004–2009) deseasonalization strategy — prevents both temporal autocorrelation leakage and deseasonalisation leakage, ensuring honest out-of-sample evaluation.

- **Continuous GRACE record reconstruction (Gap G4):** The ~11-month gap between GRACE and GRACE-FO (Jul 2017–May 2018) is typically excluded or linearly interpolated, truncating trend analysis during a critical drought period. Our **RF-based covariate-driven gap-filling** exploits the full information content of coincident hydroclimate fields ($P$, $ET$, $Q$, $T$) to reconstruct this gap, yielding a continuous 213-month TWS record for unbiased trend estimation.
