# Highlights

- A novel **Twin Random Forest Attribution Framework** disentangles natural hydroclimate variability from anthropogenic water abstraction as drivers of Terrestrial Water Storage (TWS) decline across the world's 103 largest river basins.

- Systematic, basin-by-basin driver attribution is performed at **unprecedented global scale** (103 basins, 0.5° × 0.5°, 213-month record), producing the first comprehensive global map of dominant TWS-decline drivers—unlike prior studies limited to individual basins or single regions.

- The **Variance Explained Gain (ΔR²)** metric, derived from contrasting a Natural Baseline Model (P, ET, Q) against a Full Anthropogenic Model (P, ET, Q, GW_abs, SW_abs), provides a basin-specific, quantitative fingerprint of human-induced water depletion intensity.

- A **forced feature-sampling strategy** deliberately compels the Random Forest ensemble to build decision trees without dominant climate variables (P, ET), overcoming the variance-magnitude imbalance that otherwise masks weak but critical anthropogenic abstraction signals (GW_abs, SW_abs).

- A **leakage-aware validation protocol** combining 3-year contiguous block cross-validation with a fixed-baseline deseasonalization strategy—prevents temporal autocorrelation leakage.
